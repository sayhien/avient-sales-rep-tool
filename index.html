The brief

The brief, and how I narrowed it

The brief asks for a tool that lets sales reps

  • 1Check sales progress
  • 2Prioritise high value deals
  • 3Spot stalled opportunities
  • 4Take fast action, without tedious manual documentation

It names no company and no industry, so the first decision was mine.

My scope

One company, one role, one job

  • One company. Avient Specialty Inks, which makes inks for textile screen printing.
  • One role. A sales rep in Singapore who visits print shops.
  • One job. Keep track of deals with the least effort.

A real company gives real limits. Avient sells directly and through distributors, and its reps also give technical help. Every industry has different deals and buyers, which is too much for a first version.

Meet Daniel Lim, sales rep

DL

Daniel Lim, 31

Sales rep, Avient Specialty Inks, Singapore

"I know the ink better than anyone. What I forget is who I owe a reply, and who has gone quiet."

  • 8 years in textile screen printing, 2 as a sales rep
  • About 40 customers and 30 open deals
  • On the road most days, working from his phone
  • WhatsApp, calls and email, in English, Mandarin and some Malay

Goals

  • Reach his yearly sales target
  • Know what to do first each day
  • Be trusted as an expert, not a price quoter

He will not accept

  • Long forms
  • Typing the same thing twice

What I learned

The best information comes up in the meeting

But there is no polite way to keep it. Typing on a phone looks like he is not listening.

Fast replies win deals

But only if he knows who is waiting for one.

Quiet customers are the easiest to win back

But nobody can see them, so old quotes are forgotten.

Selling is personal, and a team effort

The record should hold who decides and what they care about, so nobody asks the customer the same question twice.

Customers may not accept being recorded

Reps in a forum say they record only with permission, and that handwritten notes build trust. Whether Singapore print shop owners agree is a guess to test.

Sources: the job description, Avient's public pages, seven sales videos, and one forum thread where field reps discuss meeting notes.

Define

How might we

  1. let Daniel pass on what he learns by talking, without breaking his conversation with the customer?
  2. turn scattered notes, chats and old quotes into a clear picture of each customer?
  3. tell Daniel which deals matter most today, in a few seconds?
  4. make quiet deals easy to see, without nagging him?
  5. show how far he is from his target, and what would move it, without a report to fill in?

Develop

23 ideas, one gate, five to build

Privacy is a gate, not a trade

Impact and effort can be traded against each other. Privacy cannot. A risky idea can break the law or a customer's trust, so any idea with a high privacy risk is parked until there is a safer version.

The core of five

  • Monday plan and top three for priorities
  • Home with the target view for progress
  • Quiet deal warnings for stalled deals
  • Capture and tidy up for fast action
  • Customer record, the base they all read from

Set aside

  • Parked for privacy: recording whole meetings, an always listening phone, smart glasses
  • Later: product tips from Avient material. The notebook photo came back in review, for phones with no pen

18 of 23 pass the gate. Scores are my first guesses.

Develop

Meet Hue, the assistant

Hue

You sell. Hue keeps track.

The name

Hue, as in colour, because Avient sells inks. It is not a human name, so nobody mistakes it for a person.

The mark

Three ink discs in blue, red and yellow. It waves when the tool opens and turns while it works.

Where it shows

Beside anything Hue wrote or picked: the top three, the week plan, drafted messages, tidied notes, suggestions and warnings.

Its limits

Hue never sends or saves by itself. When it is unsure, it says "Please check". Daniel always has the last word.

Why give it a face

Daniel should feel that someone has his back. A named helper with a visible mark also makes it clear which words are his and which are the tool's.

Deliver · for the technical reviewer

Under the hood, in plain words

1. Capture

Talk: speech becomes text. Write: pen strokes become text. Photo: text is read from the picture. Type: as it is.

2. Tidy up

A language model reads the note and fills five fields: customer, what happened, next step, date and deal stage. It flags what it is unsure about.

3. Check

Daniel sees the fields and his original words, fixes anything wrong, and saves. Nothing is saved without him.

4. Rules

Top three: value, chance to close, days waiting. Quiet: days since contact against the normal wait for that stage.

Weak signal

Notes are kept on the device and sent when the signal returns. Speech to text on the device would let him capture with no signal at all.

Mixed languages

His speech mixes English, Mandarin and Singlish. Accuracy here is the biggest technical risk, so wrong guesses must be easy to fix.

Privacy

It records his voice only, and asks before recording a customer. Customer details are personal data under Singapore law.

Android and iPhone

The floating button suits Android. On iPhone it would sit in the tab bar. Reading the call list is stricter on iPhone.

What the prototype fakes: voice, pen and photo play sample notes, and nothing is really sent. The tidy up of typed notes is real but simple. An engineer should confirm the phone limits.

The test I would run next

Six tasks, on their own phone

  1. 1It is Monday morning. What would you do first, and why?
  2. 2How far are you from your target, and what would move it?
  3. 3You just left Ace Tees. Record what happened in under a minute.
  4. 4Which customers have gone quiet? Deal with one of them.
  5. 5Find what you promised Jasmine, and whether she pays on time.
  6. 6The text is too small in the sun. Fix it.

Who and how

Five field sales reps, 30 minutes each, thinking aloud. One print shop owner for the questions about recording.

What I record

Done without help, time taken, mistakes, and how sure they felt. Pass: 4 of 5 reps finish each task alone.

What I expect to be wrong about

  • Is the red and yellow in Hue's mark read as a warning?
  • Do they check the fields marked "Please check"?
  • Do they find "hold to talk"?
  • Is the weather line useful, or noise?

Status: not run yet. So far the prototype has been through five design reviews, not a test with users.

What could go wrong, and what comes next

Edge cases I designed for

  • Quiet is not always a problem. A trial can take weeks, so the wait depends on the stage of the deal.
  • Too many alerts. Few warnings, each dismissed in one tap. Hue learns what is normal for a customer.
  • Wrong guesses. "Please check" chips, his original words kept, and one tap to fix.
  • The same customer twice. A new record warns if the company is already there.
  • Recording. His voice only. Never a customer without permission.

Not built yet

  • Two sellers, one customer: show who last spoke to the shop, Daniel or a distributor.
  • Old quotes worth another try, with a message ready.
  • Email and missed calls added for him, with his permission.
  • WhatsApp chats he chooses to share.
  • A simple view for his manager.

First thing I would do

Run the test with five reps, then fix the top three problems before adding anything.
